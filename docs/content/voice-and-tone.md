# Zerado — voice & tone, for people writing copy after this page ships

The full standard is `brand-manual.md` §8. This is the pocket version, with the situations
that actually came up while writing the landing page.

## The three words, and what they are not

| We are | We are not |
|---|---|
| **Dry** — the fact is the point | Sarcastic, or bubbly |
| **Concrete** — numbers, names, specifics | Vague, or jargon-padded |
| **Confident** — direct, no hedging | Boastful, or timid |

The audience is people who live in a terminal. They have a finely-tuned detector for marketing
language, they will forgive niche, and they will not forgive hype.

## Do / don't, drawn from real Zerado situations

**1 · Describing what the product does**
- ✗ "Experience the power of a next-generation, blazing-fast CLI-first gaming ecosystem."
- ✓ "It's a text program. It starts instantly, works offline, and your library is one file
  you own."
- Three verifiable claims beat six adjectives. This audience checks.

**2 · A price is in budget but above its historical low**
- ✗ "🔥 This game is a STEAL at $15! Grab it before it's gone!"
- ✓ "$15, and in budget. It's been $8. Maybe wait."
- Zerado's actual value proposition here is telling you *not* to buy. Fake urgency destroys
  the trust the whole feature depends on.

**3 · Marking a game finished**
- ✗ "Congratulations on this incredible achievement! You're a true champion!"
- ✓ "Zerado. 41 hours. Sixth this year."
- The moment carries itself. Three facts land harder than an exclamation mark.

**4 · Nothing is downloadable yet (the whole page is pre-launch)**
- ✗ "Try Zerado today — download now!"
- ✓ "Nothing to install yet. Tell us you're in, and we'll tell you when there is."
- Say what stage the product is actually at. A waitlist framed honestly reads as early, not
  as broken.

**5 · The community layer costs money later**
- ✗ "We're SO excited to announce our amazing new social features — coming soon!"
- ✓ "These run on servers, and servers cost money — so this part will need a premium account
  or a donation once it exists."
- State the cost before anyone asks. Up front reads as respect; discovered later reads as a
  catch. And disclosure is not an ask — never follow this sentence with a button.

**6 · Steam needs a public profile, and that's a real limitation**
- ✗ "Oops! Something went wrong. Please try again later."
- ✓ "Steam only returns a library if the profile is public, or if the key belongs to that
  same account. If a profile is private, Steam won't share the list — that's a Steam setting,
  not something Zerado can work around."
- Name what happened, why, and whether it's on us or the reader. "Something went wrong" is
  the one sentence this audience cannot act on.

**7 · A feature that isn't built yet**
- ✗ "Powerful recommendations, coming soon!"
- ✓ "Phase 3 — Recommendations & Budget. What to buy, and whether to wait. Planned."
- The roadmap format *is* the honesty mechanism. A phase name, a plain-language line, and a
  status marker that never says "done" until it is.

**8 · Introducing a Portuguese word**
- ✗ Leaving *zerado* or *sinopse* unglossed, assuming the reader will infer it.
- ✗ Explaining it more than once — a glossary makes the page read like homework.
- ✓ One clause, at the first appearance, then used plainly ever after: "*zerado* — Portuguese
  for beaten: finished, cleared, 100%'d."

## Words this brand never uses

Banned outright, no exceptions found while writing this page:
- **Hype adjectives:** revolutionary, seamless, unleash, game-changing, next-generation,
  cutting-edge, blazing-fast, powerful (as a standalone claim), amazing, incredible.
- **Exclamation marks.** No situation in this product needs one — not a sync, not a purchase
  verdict, not a *zerado*.
- **Emoji in product copy.** State is carried by colour + glyph + label (brand-manual §4.3);
  emoji carry nothing and undercut that system.
- **"Gamer."** Never used to describe the reader.
- **Filler openers:** "In today's fast-paced world," "As we all know," "It's no secret that,"
  "Now more than ever." Every one wastes the most valuable line on the page.
- **Vague quantities.** "A lot of hours," "tons of games," "many players." Say the number, or
  cut the claim.
- **Thesaurus-inflation.** "Utilize" for use, "leverage" for use, "empower" for help. Plain
  verbs read as more confident, not less.

## The three awkward moments this page has to hold, and how

**1 · Nothing to download yet.**
Say the stage plainly and once, then move on — don't apologise for it repeatedly, and don't
bury it. "Nothing to install yet" is a complete sentence; it doesn't need "unfortunately" in
front of it or "but soon!" after it.

**2 · The community costs money, eventually.**
State the cost before it's asked. Never attach a button, a price, or a tier name to the
statement — there isn't one yet, and inventing one to look more finished is worse than saying
"not decided."

**3 · Steam needs a public profile.**
This is a real limitation of a third-party service, not a Zerado bug, and the copy says so
directly: name what's required, name why, and stop. Don't soften it with "unfortunately" or
oversell the workaround, because there isn't one.

## One test that catches most drafts before they ship

Read the sentence back and ask: **could a competitor's marketing team have written this
exact sentence?** If yes, it's generic — cut it or replace the adjective with a fact. Every
sentence on this page that survived that test is the one doing real work.
